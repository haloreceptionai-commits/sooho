- Blender set kits use an offline checksum-pinned CC0 subset separate from the legacy object library; this avoids online downloads during rendering and regressions in existing engines.
- Set definitions contain only whitelisted asset identities and numeric transforms, never scripts or external paths; this keeps future AI shot direction declarative.
- Normalize imported GLB roots in world-space with feet at ground level, retaining child transforms; this prevents sideways, buried, or exploding multipart models.
- Architectural detail is deterministic engine-owned geometry in set_detail.py, not AI scripts; this makes masonry, timber and weather reproducible across shots.
- Still acceptance audits explicitly prohibit motion inference and join judge responses by shot ID; repeated images and reordered responses must not fabricate evidence.
- Character joint targets live in a pure deterministic motion module, with rendered segments sharing endpoints and held props parented to wrists; this allows per-frame continuity tests without Blender and prevents detached hand-held objects.
- Unusual story props use bounded object_specs identities and engine-owned story_objects geometry; this permits moving mechanisms without executing AI-authored code or accepting external asset paths.
- Object animation uses pure object_motion trajectories and Blender per-frame verification of the evaluated meshes; this catches detached mechanism parts, hidden intact explosion bodies and invalid gravity paths before rendering.- Shot plans come from Codex but are accepted only by shot_director.py, which re-derives spoken concepts itself and compiles to schema-validated shots; this keeps model output declarative and prevents unseen or invented on-screen content.
- Effects, crowds and camera shake/whip are declarative shot fields whose motion comes from pure effects.py trajectories, with smoke, dust and fire as noise-carved volumes; this keeps effects testable, word-timed and free of AI-authored code.
- Sound effects come from sound_cues.py, which derives each cue from the same motion rules as the picture and snaps it to a frame; sound_mix.py adds the voice untouched and masters with one measured gain plus a true-peak limiter. This keeps sound in sync without AI guessing and protects narration.

- The isolated Blender benchmark suite keeps evidence-bound script audits and full-video release gates separate from production; only complete aligned renders, benchmark parity, recovery checks and owner approval may enable a renderer, because preparatory tests are not launch evidence.
- Advanced shot planning lives in art_director.py: pure deterministic rules turn spoken words into location, benchmark-length setups, a never-repeating camera grammar, literal body actions, herds/crowds, whitelisted props and word-timed effects, all schema-validated; this gives editorial variety without executing model output, and bridge falls back to legacy compile_shot if planning fails.
- Advanced shots carry a free-text `location` and phrase-defined objects (src/lib/object-resolution.ts): a library model or built-in prop is used only on a whole-phrase match, else a painted `card/<id>`; art_director picks a kit only when the location names that place type, otherwise the `neutral` sky+ground set, and reframes until every painted object is in frame; why: keyword/closest-kit matching showed wrong places and objects.
- Advanced places come from a per-scene place spec (manifest v3 `place`: terrain/time/weather/dressing ids from place-registry.json, shared by src/lib/place-spec.ts, build-manifest.py and place_spec.py) built as real 3D terrain by place_modules.py; an invalid place stops the job before rendering, and World Pack painted backdrops/keyword kit matching remain only for older jobs without `place`; why: painted cards and neutral voids drew empty or wrong worlds.
- A place-registry dressing entry is usable only when registry-audit.json holds a passing registry_check.py local render for its exact definition hash; why: the AI must never be offered an undrawable prop.
- Animal identity is one whole-word list (blender/animals.json + animals.py) read by both the app and the renderer; why: separate substring regexes hid paid painted animals (oxen) and misread people like "senator" as animals.
